We have received several letters lately
from porsons interested in the tax snit
between Stark and Billings counties.
Briefly, the matter stands as follows—
At the last session of the Dakota legist
lnture, a bill was passed, authorizing
aiiy organized county to tax for all pur
poses, adjacent unorganized counties.
Also it gave authority to the organized
county to tax any unorganized county
which was nearer to it than to any other
organized county. Under this law, Stark
county assessed and taxed Billings coun
ty before we Were organized.. The tax
amounted to nearly thirteen mills «f
which three mills was territorial tax.
All the rest was for school, bridge and
road taxes Ac., for Stark county. Our
tax-payers readily conceded the justice
of the territorial tax and have stood
ready- at any and all times to pay it, if
it could have been separated from, the
other subjocts for taxation under the
bill. They can in ho wise, however, see
the justice of being taxed to pay for the
schools, roads or bridges of Stark county.
Naturally our tax-payers, seeing the un
justice of the law, refused to pay the
tax. Stark county, being authorized by
the law, as naturally took measures to
collect the tax. Our citizens then met
and decided to fight the collection and ap
pointed T. R. Selmes.of Mandan as at
torney. Mr: Selmes gave his opinion
that the law was unconstitutional and
had an injunction issueti on. Stark coun
ty, restraining the further collection of
taxeB from Billings until the constitu
tionality of the law could be: decided by
the courts. This is the present status of
the matter. Stark county has given
every evidence of desiring fair play, and
will readily acquiesco in thej decision
wliich we confidently believe will be
adverse to the constitutionality of the
law. The case will come up at the Au
gust term of the circuit court and no
matter which side wins, it will donbtless
be appoaled. It can then come before
the supreme court in October iu Dead
wood or in November in Bismarck. We
do not think that the case will then be
