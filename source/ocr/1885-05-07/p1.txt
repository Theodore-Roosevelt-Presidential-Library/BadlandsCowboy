The secretary of the Interior, according
to instructions of the Montana associa
tion, has been earnestly petitioned by the
president and secretary of the associa
tion, to see that the Montana Indians
are properly cared for. Almost all the
difficulty ever had with the Indians was
due to the fact that the government
promised to provide for them and thenr
through Indians agents, almost starved
them, forcing them in many instances to
cat their horses and dogs. In other
words, although the intention of the
government was good, tho Indians have
experienced a perfoctly unreliable policy,
being promised things that were never
fulfilled. It is easy then to see how so
many difficulties have arisen with the
Indians. Smarting under a sense of
wrong, they have tried, in their own
way, to right it. We are not over
friendly to tho Indiaus but we are more
an enemy to the policy the government
has adopted toward them and still more
an enemy to the miserably ineffectual
way the government has of carrying out
its policy. By all means' Tet ns have a
change. Anything will be an improve
ment over the present system.
