MELTED BUTTER.

This being the basis of many sauces,'
its preparation is important. Put into
a small saucepan two ounces of soft,
fresh butter and a large tablespoonful
of flour. Mix these well together,
wnile cold, with a wooden spoon, and
then add about half a pint of cold wa
ter, nearly a teaspoonful of salt and a
little white pepper. Set this over the
fire and stir it continually until nearly
boiling. The ingredients ought to be
perfectly blended but the butter, from
bad quality or other, cause, will fre
quently float as oil on the surfaoe.
This imperfection may be partially
remedied by adding a very little cold
water, and then pouring the sauce
quickly from the saucepan into a basin
and back again several times. When
prepared for other sauces less- water
may be used if the flavoring additions
are fluid, and rather more where these,
as in egg sauce, have a thickening
tendency. Acids, when mixed with
melted butter, being apt to make it
"oil," must be well stirred in. When
alight colored sauce is required, milk
may be substituted for water in the
melted butter.

rind and leaves being left on while toil
ing will keep in the flavor of the fruit.
Cut the pineapples into ronnd slioes

a round hole in the middle of every
slice. Weigh them, and to each pound

On this place a layer of pineapple
slices, then a layer of sugar, then one
of pineapple, and.so on.
..until the. pine
apple slices pre all covejred, finishing

the scum ceases to rise. Then pour it
hot upon the pineapple. While still
warm cover the jars closely and paste
paper over them.
