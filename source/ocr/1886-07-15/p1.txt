To the average reader, it must seem
absolutely impossibe that the soldiers
who are after the Apaches in Arizona,
can be brave men. We have continually
seen articles in eastern papers, drawing
unfavorable comment from the fact that
a thousand soldiers have for more than a
year been chasing a hundred Apaches
and have virtually accomplished noth
ing. When we think that the Bad Lands
are almost a level prairie in comparison
with he country in which the Apaches
are fighting, that they know every inch
of it, that they are as well or better
armed than the soldiers, that they can
on foot run clear away from a troop of
cavalry in a day, that they leave almost
no trail, that they are constantly sub
dividing into bauds of from three to twen
ty and striking where they are least ex
pected, tlrat they can, lire in' luxury
where soldiers would' starve and-that
man for man there is no body of troops
in the world a match for them on their
chosen battle ground, the results of the
campaign are more easily explained.
About the only feasible way now seems
to he to furnish a sufficient number of
troops so that every water hole could be
guarded and the Apaches kept moving so
that exhaustion would finally compel
them to surrender.
