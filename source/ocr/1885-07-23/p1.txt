Joseph Nimmo, Jr., chief of the bureau
of statistics and author of tho recent
Report in regard to the Range and*
Ranch cattle business jof the United
States" is already collecting the material
for a popular work on the subject The
government edition ol.his work is ex
hausted and the call for it has been so
large that it shows a good opening for
the work Mr. Nimmo has in contempla
tion. It is afield entirely unoccupied
except by newspapers and we wish the
author every success in his undertaking.
Mr. Nimmo informs us that he intends
soon making a tour of inspection of the
range country and will spend s6me time
in the Bad Lands.

According to the latest reports there
are very nearly, if not quite 50,000,000
cattle in the United States. Of these
Texas stands at the head of the list with
9,000,000. Iowa comes next with over
3,500,000. Then comes Missouri, New
York, Illinois and Kansas in the order
named with over 2,000,000 each. Ohio,
Nebraska, Pennsylvania, Indiana, Wis
consin, Wyoming and Indian Territory,
following with numbers from a few over
a million in the latter to nearly two
million in the former. Montana has
nearly 800,000 and Dakota has nearly
400,000. Little Rhody foots the list with
35,000.

The cattle raisers of Dakota have an
advantage over those of Wyoming which
is scarcely valued as it should be. This
is in the matter of freight rates. What
ever can be said in reference to rates
charged by the Northern Pacific in other
matters, it must be acknowledged that
their rates on cattle are extremely low.
In car-load lots, according to Nimmo's
report, the tariff is at the rate of fifty
three cents a hundred miles from Chi
cago to New York, the rate on the Union
Pecific is sixty cents and that on the
Northern Pacific is fifty-six cents. When
we notice that there are many roads
competing for the business from Chicago
to New York and that there is no compe
tition on the Northern Pacific, it is man
ifest that the latter is giving every
encouragement to cattle raising. In
Dakota especially, as we started to re
mark, do these rates benefit our cowmen.
The average rate, per head from Dakota
shipping points to Chicago is $4.88. The
rate from Wyoming ^points is $7.22.
Thus the Dakota cattleman gains an
additional profit of $2.34 per head over
his Wyoming brother. In dealing with
large herds this is a very neat figure to
save.
