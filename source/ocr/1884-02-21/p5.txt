°A11 indications and recent develop
ments point to the speedy opening of the
freight route between this point and
Deadwood. It is now almost as good as a
sottled fact. Whether a route is estab
lished from Dickinson or not is a matter
of small importance, except for the short
lived opposition it.

will offer. Recent ar­

ticles in the Dickinson Press and Minne
sela Boomer state that the contemplated
route from Dickinson tb Deadwood runs
through Minnesela. This fact alone
proves that the route from Dickinson
will either never be started or else will
be short lived. During wet seasons, forty
miles of this route is absolutely impass
ible. All. along the Moreau and to Min
nesela is one vast bed of gumbo. There
is no bottom to- this, and wagons will
mire over the axles. At the present
writing it is impossible to ride a horse
over part of the route. Great cracks are
found evcarywhere and horses are in con
stant danger of stepping into them and
breaking their legs.

As we have before stated, the. route
from Dickinson is: at best a poor One.
For a large part of the distance it must
cross the head waters of the Heart, Can
nonball and Moreau rivers. These will
require many bridges, and, in fact, al
most the whole route from Dickinson to
Deadwood would have to be bridged to
escape the gumbo. The route from here,
on t^e other hand, is entirely' clear of
these head waters and traverses the level,
grassy divide between them and the Lit
tle Missouri valley. Very little gumbo is
encountered and the route from begin
ning to end presents no serious obstacles.
The worst part of the road is right here
in the- Bad Lands, and this morning a
gang of men was set to work to "make a
good road out to the divide. This will
soon be completed and tho route will be
in condition.

This route is for summer, bnt in winter
the Little Missouri river and valley fur
nish a route that would be a delight to
any freighter. Two routes, each vastly
superior than the one from Dickinson,
would really seem to indicate that noser
rions opposition will be felt in that di
rection.

tation for 250,000 pounds of freight, was

route to bring all the horses, mules and
wagons to this point. Indeed, some have
already stairted and will be here in a few
days. T.- G.- Jones of Deadwood, will
also be in town in a few days. Ho has
oyer four hundred head of horses and
a hundred mules that lie will bring here
as soon^as preparations can be made.
The Jones Bros, have already contracted
for hauling all the bullion of the Home
stake, mine, which will in itself support a
large freighting outfit Many Deadwood
merchants liave also been consulted in
reference to their freight and have guar
anteed it to our line as soon as it is
started. The popular opinion seems to
be that in some way the freight line is:
connected with the government or rail
road This is entirely false. It is mere
ly a private enterprise and one could be
started.from:every towp on the N. P. if it
had sufficient backing. This is the rea
son why our. route is next to a certainty'
The Marquis has written from Washing
ton that the freight route must be estab
lished^ here at all events, whether there
is opposition at Dickinson or not
.The mail route is entirely an outside
matter. This is one of the principal
reasons for the Marquis* Washington
trip, however, and we will doubtless
soon Mve that also.

The refrigerator cars of the N. P. R. C.
Co. are marvels of neatness and simplici
ty. They: are considered eqnal, if not
superior to any other refrigerator car
made. The cars are very soli(Uy built
and are air-tight except for ventilation.
They are of double thickness and are
built for heavy use. The ice boxes are at
either end, the capacity of both being
from three to five tons. -The boxes are
elevated from the floor and are provided
with waste troughs and pipes. Under
the boxes is room to pack nearts, livers,
etc. Long iron rods, running lengthwise
of the car, are used to hang the beef on.
It is first cut into quarters, and all the
quarters numbered. The quarters are
then separated, for instance, the quarters
of No. 1 occupy the four corners of the
car the quarters of No. 2 come next,and
so on till-the car is filled. A load con
sists of the quarters of from thirty to
forty dressed beef. There are now twen
ty-four refrigerator cars belonging to the
company, and doubtless others will be
needed before long. The car. is the in
vention .of Mr. C.W. Sleeper, now vice
president of the company.
